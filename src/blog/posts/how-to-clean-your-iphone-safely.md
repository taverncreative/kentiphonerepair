## How to Clean Your iPhone Safely

Your iPhone goes everywhere with you. It sits on restaurant tables, rides in your pocket alongside keys and coins, gets touched by greasy fingers after lunch, and pressed against your face during calls. Studies have found that the average smartphone carries more bacteria than a toilet seat, which is a sobering thought. Regular cleaning keeps your phone hygienic, looking good, and functioning properly.

However, iPhones are precision devices with sensitive coatings and delicate components. Cleaning them incorrectly can cause scratches, damage the oleophobic (fingerprint-resistant) coating, or push debris into openings where it causes problems. Here is a thorough guide to cleaning your iPhone safely, covering every part of the device.

## Cleaning the Screen

The screen is the part you touch most, and it is also the most visible surface, so keeping it clean makes a noticeable difference.

### What to use

- **A lint-free microfibre cloth.** This is the gold standard for screen cleaning. The same type of cloth you would use to clean glasses or camera lenses works perfectly. Apple includes one with some products, and they are inexpensive to buy.
- **Slightly dampened cloth for stubborn marks.** If a dry cloth is not removing a smudge, lightly dampen the cloth with plain water. Wring it out thoroughly so it is just barely damp, not wet.
- **70% isopropyl alcohol wipes.** Apple has confirmed that these are safe for iPhone screens. They are useful for disinfecting the surface. Clorox Disinfecting Wipes are also Apple-approved.

### What to avoid

- **Paper towels or tissues.** These are abrasive at a microscopic level and will gradually scratch the screen and wear away the oleophobic coating.
- **Household glass cleaners like Windex.** These contain ammonia and other chemicals that strip the oleophobic coating from iPhone screens.
- **Compressed air directly on the screen.** The force can push particles across the screen surface, causing scratches.
- **Abrasive cleaners or scouring agents.** This should go without saying, but no abrasive cleaning products should ever touch your phone screen.

### Cleaning technique

1. Power off your iPhone or at least lock the screen.
2. Remove the case if you have one.
3. Gently wipe the screen with the microfibre cloth using straight, even strokes. Avoid circular motions, as these can create visible swirl marks on some screen protectors.
4. For stubborn marks, slightly dampen the cloth and repeat.
5. If disinfecting, use a 70% isopropyl alcohol wipe and let the screen air dry.

## Cleaning the Charging Port

A dirty charging port is the number one cause of charging problems. Lint, dust, and debris accumulate gradually and can prevent the cable from seating properly.

### What to use

- **A wooden or plastic toothpick.** Gently scrape along the inside walls and bottom of the port to dislodge compacted lint. Wood and plastic are soft enough not to damage the metal contacts.
- **A new, dry, soft-bristle toothbrush.** Gently brush inside the port to loosen debris.
- **A torch.** Shine a light into the port before and after cleaning to see what you are working with and to check your progress.

### What to avoid

- **Metal tools like pins or needles.** These can scratch or bend the delicate contact pins inside the port, causing permanent damage.
- **Compressed air at close range.** While a quick burst from a distance can help, blasting compressed air directly into the port at close range can damage internal components.
- **Liquids of any kind.** Never spray or drip any liquid into the charging port.

### Cleaning technique

1. Power off your iPhone.
2. Use a torch to inspect the port and identify any visible debris.
3. Gently insert the wooden toothpick and scrape along the bottom of the port, working from back to front to draw debris out.
4. Follow up with the soft toothbrush to sweep out loosened particles.
5. Inspect again with the torch. Clean contacts should appear shiny and metallic.

## Cleaning the Speakers and Microphone

The speaker grilles on the bottom of your iPhone and the earpiece at the top can accumulate dust and debris that muffles sound quality.

### What to use

- **A soft-bristle toothbrush.** Gently brush across the speaker grilles to dislodge surface debris.
- **Painter's tape or mounting putty.** Press a small piece of adhesive tape or putty over the speaker grilles and peel away. The adhesive picks up debris that brushing misses.

### What to avoid

- **Inserting anything into the speaker openings.** The speaker mesh is delicate and can be damaged or pushed inward by poking with sharp objects.
- **Blowing into the speakers.** Your breath contains moisture that can damage internal components.

## Cleaning the Camera Lenses

Smudged camera lenses result in hazy, unfocused photos. A quick clean can transform your photo quality.

### What to use

- A clean, dry microfibre cloth. The same one you use for the screen works perfectly.
- For stubborn residue, slightly dampen the cloth with water.

### What to avoid

- Lens cleaning solutions designed for professional camera equipment. These are generally safe, but the simplest approach is just water on a microfibre cloth.
- T-shirts, jeans, and other fabric. While it is tempting to give the lens a quick wipe on your shirt, clothing fibres can be surprisingly abrasive.

## Cleaning Your Case

If you use a phone case, it deserves regular cleaning too. Cases trap dirt, moisture, and bacteria between themselves and the phone.

### Silicone cases

Wash with warm water and a small amount of mild hand soap. Rinse thoroughly and dry completely before putting it back on your phone.

### Clear plastic cases

These tend to yellow over time. Warm soapy water helps with general cleaning, but yellowing is a chemical reaction with UV light and cannot be reversed. If your clear case has turned significantly yellow, it may be time for a replacement.

### Leather cases

Wipe with a damp microfibre cloth only. Do not use soap, alcohol, or any cleaning products on leather, as these can stain or dry out the material.

### Fabric cases

Use a lint roller or tape to remove surface debris. For deeper cleaning, gently hand wash with cold water and mild detergent, then air dry completely before reuse.

## How Often Should You Clean Your iPhone?

- **Screen:** A quick wipe with a microfibre cloth daily or whenever you notice smudges.
- **Full disinfection:** Once or twice a week, or more frequently if you have been in public spaces like gyms or hospitals.
- **Charging port:** Check monthly and clean as needed, or whenever you notice charging inconsistencies.
- **Speakers:** Every few weeks, or when you notice reduced sound quality.
- **Case:** Weekly for hygiene, especially if you remove the case regularly and notice trapped debris.
