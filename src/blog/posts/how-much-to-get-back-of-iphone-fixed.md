## How Much to Get the Back of an iPhone Fixed?

Since Apple introduced glass backs on the iPhone 8 in 2017, rear glass damage has become one of the most common repair requests we receive. The glass back enables wireless charging but comes at a cost: it is just as vulnerable to drops as the front screen. If you have cracked the back of your iPhone, you are probably wondering what it will cost to fix. The answer depends on several factors.

### Why Back Glass Repairs Are Different

Repairing the back glass of an iPhone is fundamentally different from replacing the front screen. On older models, the rear glass was bonded directly to the phone's chassis with extremely strong adhesive, making removal difficult and time-consuming. Apple's own repair process for these models historically involved replacing the entire rear enclosure, which is why their pricing was so high.

On newer models, Apple has gradually made the back glass easier to replace. The iPhone 14 and later models were designed with a more accessible rear glass panel, which has brought repair costs down somewhat. However, it is still a more involved process than a front screen replacement.

### Cost Factors

Several things influence the cost of a back glass repair:

**Your iPhone model.** This is the single biggest factor. The cost varies significantly across different models:

- Older models (iPhone 8, iPhone X, iPhone XS) tend to be less expensive for parts, but the labour is more intensive because the glass is harder to remove.
- Mid-range models (iPhone 11, iPhone 12, iPhone 13) fall in the middle for both parts and labour.
- Newer and Pro models (iPhone 14 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 16 Pro) have more expensive components, particularly if the glass integrates with other elements like the camera housing.

**Repair method.** There are two main approaches:
- **Full back glass replacement** where the cracked glass is carefully removed and replaced with a new panel. This is the standard approach for most professional repairs.
- **Laser removal** which is a newer technique using a laser machine to separate the adhesive bond between the glass and the frame. This method is faster and often produces a cleaner result, but requires specialist equipment.

**Parts quality.** As with screen repairs, the quality of replacement back glass varies. Higher-quality replacements that match the original colour, finish, and fit will cost more than generic alternatives, but they look better and last longer.

**Colour matching.** Some colours are harder to source than others. Standard colours like black and white are readily available, but limited edition or seasonal colours may be more expensive or difficult to find as exact matches.

### Typical Price Ranges

While we always recommend getting a specific quote for your model, here are some general price ranges to give you an idea:

- **iPhone 8 / 8 Plus:** These older models can be more labour-intensive due to the strongly bonded glass, but parts are relatively inexpensive.
- **iPhone X / XS / XS Max:** Similar to the iPhone 8 in terms of repair complexity, with slightly higher parts costs.
- **iPhone 11 series:** Generally mid-range pricing for both parts and labour.
- **iPhone 12 to 13 series:** Parts are widely available, and the repair process is well-established.
- **iPhone 14 and later:** Apple redesigned the internal layout to make back glass replacement easier, which has helped keep labour costs reasonable despite higher parts prices for Pro models.

In general, a professional back glass replacement is significantly cheaper than Apple's own pricing and dramatically cheaper than buying a new phone.

### Apple's Own Pricing

Apple's out-of-warranty pricing for back glass damage has historically been steep. For many models, Apple's repair cost for rear glass approaches the cost of buying a refurbished phone of the same model. This is because Apple often replaces the entire rear enclosure rather than just the glass panel.

If you have AppleCare+, back glass damage is covered under the accidental damage provision with a service fee, which makes Apple's service more reasonable. Without AppleCare+, third-party repair is almost always the more cost-effective option.

### DIY Risks

You might find DIY back glass repair kits online, but this is one repair that is genuinely difficult to do yourself. Here is why:

- **Strong adhesive.** The glass is bonded to the frame with industrial-strength adhesive that requires either a heat gun or a laser to soften. Excessive heat can damage the battery and other internal components.
- **Fragile components underneath.** The wireless charging coil, NFC antenna, and other delicate components sit directly beneath the glass. Damaging these during removal adds cost to the repair.
- **Glass shards.** Removing cracked glass generates sharp fragments that can cause injury and can damage internal components if they fall inside the phone.
- **Cosmetic finish.** Getting a clean result with proper adhesion and no gaps requires experience and the right tools. A poorly fitted back glass panel looks obvious and can compromise the phone's water resistance.
- **Camera area.** The back glass around the camera module requires particular care to avoid damaging the lenses or the camera housing.

The potential savings from a DIY attempt are small compared to the risk of making the problem worse and ending up with a more expensive professional repair.

### Professional Repair Is the Smart Choice

A professional repair ensures the job is done properly with quality parts, appropriate tools, and the experience to handle any complications. The back glass is removed cleanly, the new panel is fitted precisely, and the phone's water resistance is maintained as far as possible.
