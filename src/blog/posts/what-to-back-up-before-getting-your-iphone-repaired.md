## What to Back Up Before Getting Your iPhone Repaired

Getting your iPhone repaired, whether it is a screen replacement, battery swap, or charging port fix, should not put your data at risk. Most common repairs do not touch your data at all. Your photos, messages, apps, and settings remain exactly where they are throughout the process. However, having a current backup before any repair is simply good practice, and there are specific types of data that deserve extra attention.

At Kent iPhone Repair, we always recommend making a backup before we arrive for your repair appointment. Here is a thorough guide to what you should back up and how to do it, so you can hand over your phone with complete peace of mind.

## Why Back Up Before a Repair?

Even though a standard screen or battery replacement does not erase data, there are rare situations where data could be at risk:

- **Unexpected complications.** In extremely rare cases, a repair can reveal a secondary issue, such as a logic board fault, that may require a factory reset to diagnose or resolve.
- **Severe damage scenarios.** If your screen is completely non-functional or the phone will not turn on, the technician may need to test the phone in ways that could, in edge cases, trigger a reset.
- **General best practice.** Backups protect you against all kinds of data loss, not just repair-related scenarios. Your phone could be lost, stolen, or suffer water damage at any time. A recent backup means you can recover everything on a new device within hours.

Think of it as insurance. You probably will not need it, but you will be very glad it exists if you do.

## iCloud Backup: The Simplest Option

iCloud backup is the most convenient way to back up your entire iPhone. It captures almost everything on your device and can restore it to any iPhone automatically.

### How to run an iCloud backup

1. Connect to Wi-Fi.
2. Go to Settings > [your name] > iCloud > iCloud Backup.
3. Make sure iCloud Backup is toggled on.
4. Tap "Back Up Now."
5. Stay connected to Wi-Fi until the backup completes.

### What iCloud backup includes

- App data and app layout
- Apple Watch backup (if applicable)
- Device settings (including Wi-Fi passwords and wallpaper)
- Home screen and app organisation
- iMessage, SMS, and MMS messages
- Photos and videos (if iCloud Photos is not already syncing them)
- Purchase history from Apple services
- Ringtones
- Visual Voicemail password

### Storage considerations

Apple provides 5GB of free iCloud storage, which is rarely enough for a full backup, especially if you have many photos and videos. If you need more space, Apple offers paid plans starting at 50GB. Alternatively, you can use the computer-based backup method described below.

## Computer Backup: The Complete Option

Backing up to a Mac or PC via Finder (macOS Catalina and later) or iTunes (Windows and older macOS) creates a complete local backup that does not require iCloud storage.

### How to back up to a computer

1. Connect your iPhone to your computer using a Lightning or USB-C cable.
2. On Mac (Catalina or later), open Finder and select your iPhone in the sidebar. On Windows or older Mac, open iTunes.
3. Click "Back Up Now."
4. For maximum security, tick "Encrypt local backup" and set a password. Encrypted backups include additional data like saved passwords, Health data, and Wi-Fi settings that unencrypted backups skip.

### Why encrypted backup matters

An encrypted backup captures data that a standard backup does not, including:

- Saved passwords and Wi-Fi credentials
- Health and fitness data
- Website history
- Call history

If any of this data matters to you, always choose encrypted backup.

## Photos and Videos: Your Most Irreplaceable Data

For most people, photos and videos are the most valuable data on their phone because they are irreplaceable. Make absolutely sure these are backed up.

### Options for backing up photos

- **iCloud Photos:** If enabled (Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos), your entire photo library syncs to iCloud automatically. Verify that the sync is up to date by opening the Photos app and scrolling to the bottom, where it shows the sync status.
- **Google Photos:** A popular alternative that offers storage for photos and videos. Install the app, sign in, and enable backup.
- **Direct transfer to computer:** Connect your iPhone to a computer and copy photos directly. On Mac, use the Photos app or Image Capture. On Windows, use the Photos app or File Explorer.
- **External storage:** If you have a Lightning or USB-C flash drive compatible with iPhones, you can copy photos to it using the Files app.

## Messages and Conversations

Text messages, iMessages, and group chats often contain important information, sentimental conversations, and shared media.

### Backing up messages

- **iCloud Messages:** Go to Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Messages and toggle it on. Your entire message history syncs to iCloud and is available on any device signed into your Apple ID.
- **iCloud or computer backup:** Both backup methods described above include your messages automatically.
- **Third-party export tools:** If you want a permanent archive of specific conversations, apps like iMazing or PhoneView can export message threads as readable files.

## Contacts

Your contact list is essential for staying connected. Fortunately, contacts are usually the easiest data to back up.

### Backing up contacts

- **iCloud Contacts:** Go to Settings > [your name] > iCloud and ensure Contacts is toggled on. Your contacts sync automatically and are available on icloud.com.
- **Google Contacts:** If your contacts are stored in a Google account, they are already backed up to Google's servers.
- **Export as vCard:** You can export all contacts from icloud.com as a .vcf file for safekeeping.

## Apps and App Data

Most apps store their data in the cloud these days, but some store data locally on your device.

### Check these apps specifically

- **Banking apps:** Most require re-authentication after any phone change, so make sure you know your login credentials.
- **Authenticator apps:** This deserves its own section (see below) because losing authenticator data can lock you out of important accounts.
- **Notes:** Ensure Notes is syncing via iCloud (Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Notes).
- **Voice Memos:** Check that these are syncing via iCloud.
- **Offline-capable apps:** Any app that stores data locally (such as downloaded maps, offline documents, or local databases) should be synced or backed up before the repair.

## Authenticator Apps: Critical

This is the one area where people most commonly get caught out. Two-factor authentication apps like Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, and Authy store security codes that you need to log into important accounts.

### What to do

- **Google Authenticator:** Recent versions support cloud backup to your Google account. Open the app and check that your accounts are synced. If you are using an older version without cloud backup, you will need to manually transfer codes or set up your accounts with backup codes.
- **Microsoft Authenticator:** Supports iCloud backup. Enable it in the app's settings.
- **Authy:** Stores codes in the cloud by default, so your codes are safe as long as you remember your Authy password.
- **Backup codes:** For critical accounts (email, banking, social media), most services provide backup codes when you set up two-factor authentication. Store these somewhere safe outside your phone, such as a password manager or a printed sheet in a secure location.

## Health Data

If you use your iPhone to track health and fitness data, including steps, heart rate, workouts, medications, and medical records, this data needs attention.

### How to protect health data

- **Encrypted backup:** Health data is only included in encrypted computer backups. If you use an unencrypted backup, your health data will not be preserved.
- **iCloud:** If Health is enabled in your iCloud settings, health data syncs to iCloud.

## A Pre-Repair Checklist

Before your Kent iPhone Repair appointment, run through this checklist:

- [ ] iCloud backup completed (check Settings > [your name] > iCloud > iCloud Backup for the last backup date)
- [ ] Photos synced or backed up separately
- [ ] iCloud Messages enabled
- [ ] Contacts syncing confirmed
- [ ] Authenticator apps backed up or backup codes saved
- [ ] Banking app login credentials noted
- [ ] Health data included in an encrypted backup
- [ ] Important notes and voice memos synced
