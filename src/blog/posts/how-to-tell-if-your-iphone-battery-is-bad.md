## How to Tell If Your iPhone Battery Is Bad

Every iPhone battery has a finite lifespan. It is not a question of whether your battery will degrade, but when. Understanding the signs of a failing battery helps you decide the right time to replace it, before it starts causing bigger problems like unexpected shutdowns, data loss, or even physical damage from swelling.

Here is a comprehensive guide to diagnosing your iPhone battery health, covering both the built-in tools Apple provides and the real-world symptoms that tell you it is time for a replacement. Whether you are in Ashford, Maidstone, Canterbury, Folkestone, or elsewhere in Kent, this information applies to every iPhone user.

## Check Battery Health in Settings

Apple has built a useful battery health monitor directly into iOS. This is the first place to look when you suspect battery problems.

### How to find it

Go to Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging. Here you will see two key pieces of information.

### Maximum Capacity

This percentage represents how much charge your battery can hold compared to when it was new. A brand-new iPhone shows 100%. Over time, this number decreases as the battery chemistry degrades through normal use.

- **90-100%:** Your battery is in good health. Any performance issues you experience are unlikely to be battery-related.
- **80-89%:** Your battery is ageing but still functional. You may notice slightly shorter battery life than when the phone was new.
- **Below 80%:** Apple considers the battery "consumed" at this point. You will likely experience noticeably shorter battery life, and iOS may enable performance management to prevent unexpected shutdowns. Replacement is recommended.

### Peak Performance Capability

Below the Maximum Capacity, there is a section about performance. If your battery is healthy, it will say "Your battery is currently supporting normal peak performance." If there have been unexpected shutdowns, you may see a message indicating that performance management has been applied. This means iOS is throttling your processor speed to compensate for the degraded battery.

## Real-World Symptoms of a Bad Battery

The Battery Health screen gives you a useful number, but the symptoms you experience in daily use tell the fuller story. Here are the telltale signs.

### Rapid battery drain

If your iPhone used to last a full day comfortably and now struggles to make it to the afternoon, the battery is likely the cause, assuming you have not changed your usage habits significantly or installed a battery-hungry app. The decline is usually gradual, which makes it easy to normalise until you realise just how often you are reaching for the charger.

### Unexpected shutdowns

This is one of the clearest signs of a failing battery. Your iPhone shows 20% or even 30% remaining, and then suddenly powers off as if the battery were completely dead. When you plug it in and restart, it may show a different percentage than before the shutdown. This happens because a degraded battery cannot deliver the peak current the processor demands during intensive tasks, causing the system to shut down as a protection measure.

### Percentage jumps

A healthy battery drains in a smooth, predictable curve. A failing battery may jump erratically, going from 50% to 25% in minutes, or sitting at one percentage for ages before dropping suddenly. These jumps indicate the battery can no longer accurately report its charge level or deliver consistent output.

### Slow charging

If your iPhone takes noticeably longer to charge than it used to, the battery may be losing its ability to accept charge efficiently. A battery that once went from empty to full in under two hours now taking three or four hours is a sign of degradation.

### Excessive heat

All batteries generate some heat during charging and intensive use, but a failing battery may produce excessive heat. If your phone feels uncomfortably warm during normal tasks like browsing or messaging, or gets hot during charging, the battery could be the issue. Excessive heat also accelerates further degradation, creating a vicious cycle.

### Physical swelling

This is the most serious symptom. If your screen is lifting away from the frame, the back panel is bulging, or you can see a gap between the screen and the chassis, you almost certainly have a swollen battery. Stop using the phone immediately, do not charge it, and seek professional replacement urgently. Swollen batteries are a safety hazard.

### Performance throttling

If your iPhone feels sluggish, apps take longer to open, animations stutter, and general responsiveness has declined, check whether performance management is active in Settings > Battery > Battery Health. iOS throttles performance when the battery can no longer support peak power demands. A new battery restores full performance.

## What Causes Battery Degradation?

Understanding the causes helps you maximise the lifespan of your current or replacement battery.

- **Charge cycles.** Every time you discharge and recharge your battery through a full 100%, that counts as one charge cycle. Apple designs batteries to retain 80% capacity after approximately 500 cycles, though real-world results vary.
- **Heat exposure.** Consistently using or charging your phone in hot conditions accelerates chemical degradation within the battery.
- **Full discharge cycles.** Regularly letting your battery drain to zero before charging stresses the battery more than keeping it between 20% and 80%.
- **Age.** Even if rarely used, lithium-ion batteries degrade over time. A phone that has sat in a drawer for three years will have a degraded battery.

## When to Replace Your Battery

The decision to replace your battery comes down to a combination of the health percentage and the symptoms you are experiencing.

**Replace soon if:**

- Maximum Capacity is below 80%.
- You are experiencing unexpected shutdowns.
- Performance management has been enabled.
- The battery is physically swelling.

**Consider replacing if:**

- Maximum Capacity is between 80-85% and you are unhappy with battery life.
- You plan to keep the phone for another year or more.
- You are experiencing some of the symptoms described above.

**No rush if:**

- Maximum Capacity is above 85%.
- Battery life meets your daily needs.
- No symptoms of degradation are present.

## Battery Replacement: What to Expect

A professional battery replacement is a straightforward procedure that takes approximately 30 minutes. The technician opens the phone, disconnects the old battery, installs a fresh one, and reassembles the device. Your data, apps, photos, and settings remain completely untouched throughout the process.

After replacement, you can expect:

- Significantly improved battery life, often dramatically so if the old battery was heavily degraded.
- Restoration of full processor performance if throttling was active.
- A battery health reading of 100% in Settings.
- Your phone feeling faster and more responsive overall.
