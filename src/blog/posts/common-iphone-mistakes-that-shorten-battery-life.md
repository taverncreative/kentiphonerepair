## Common iPhone Mistakes That Shorten Battery Life

Every iPhone battery degrades over time. That is an unavoidable fact of lithium-ion battery chemistry. However, the rate at which your battery degrades is heavily influenced by how you use and charge your phone. Many iPhone owners unknowingly make mistakes every day that accelerate battery wear, leading to a phone that struggles to last through the afternoon when it used to comfortably make it through the entire day. Here are the most common mistakes and what you can do about them.

### Overnight Charging: The Myth and the Reality

The question of whether overnight charging damages your battery is one of the most debated topics among iPhone users. The answer is nuanced.

Modern iPhones include a feature called Optimised Battery Charging, introduced in iOS 13. When enabled, your iPhone learns your daily charging pattern and holds the charge at 80 percent for most of the night, only topping up to 100 percent shortly before you typically wake up. This is because lithium-ion batteries experience the most stress when held at very high charge levels for extended periods.

**The mistake:** Disabling Optimised Battery Charging or using chargers and cables that do not support the feature properly. Some third-party cables and chargers may not communicate correctly with the phone, preventing this feature from working as intended.

**What to do:** Make sure Optimised Battery Charging is enabled in Settings, then Battery, then Battery Health and Charging. Use a quality charger and cable, ideally MFi-certified, and try to charge in the same location at the same time each night so the phone can learn your routine.

### Exposing Your Phone to Extreme Temperatures

Temperature is one of the biggest enemies of lithium-ion batteries, and this is a mistake that many people do not even realise they are making.

**Heat damage** is particularly destructive. Leaving your phone on a car dashboard in summer, using it while it charges with a heavy case on, or leaving it in direct sunlight can all push the battery temperature well above its comfort zone. High temperatures accelerate the chemical degradation inside the battery permanently.

**Cold damage** is less permanent but still harmful. In very cold weather, your battery may temporarily lose significant capacity, causing the phone to shut down unexpectedly even when it shows charge remaining. While the capacity returns when the phone warms up, repeated exposure to extreme cold can accelerate long-term degradation.

**What to do:**
- Never leave your phone in a hot car, even for a few minutes
- Remove thick cases while charging if the phone gets warm
- Avoid using your phone in direct sunlight for extended periods
- In winter, keep your phone in an inside pocket close to your body rather than an outside coat pocket
- If your phone feels hot, let it cool down before continuing to use it

### Letting the Battery Drain to Zero

There is a persistent myth that you should fully discharge your iPhone battery before recharging it. This advice comes from the era of nickel-cadmium batteries, which did suffer from a "memory effect." Lithium-ion batteries are completely different.

**The mistake:** Regularly draining your battery to zero percent before plugging it in. Deep discharges put significant stress on a lithium-ion battery and can reduce its overall lifespan.

**What to do:** Try to keep your battery between roughly 20 and 80 percent most of the time. Plug in when you get to around 20 to 30 percent, and do not worry about unplugging before it reaches 100 percent. The occasional full cycle is fine, but making it a daily habit is not ideal.

### Ignoring Background App Activity

Many iPhone users have dozens of apps running in the background, constantly refreshing content, tracking location, and consuming power. While iOS is reasonably good at managing background activity, some apps are worse offenders than others.

**The mistake:** Never checking which apps are consuming the most battery and allowing everything to refresh in the background unchecked.

**What to do:**
- Go to Settings, then Battery, and check which apps are using the most power
- Disable Background App Refresh for apps that do not need it (Settings, then General, then Background App Refresh)
- Review Location Services and set most apps to "While Using" rather than "Always"
- Be cautious with social media apps, which are often the biggest background consumers
- Turn off unnecessary notifications from apps you rarely use

### Keeping Screen Brightness at Maximum

Your iPhone's display is its single biggest power consumer. Running it at full brightness all day significantly reduces battery life.

**The mistake:** Disabling auto-brightness or manually keeping the screen at maximum brightness.

**What to do:**
- Enable auto-brightness in Settings, then Accessibility, then Display and Text Size
- Use Dark Mode where possible, especially on OLED-equipped iPhones where true black pixels are actually switched off, saving power
- Reduce the auto-lock timer so the screen turns off more quickly when not in use
- Consider reducing the brightness manually in environments where full brightness is not needed

### Using the Wrong Charger

Not all chargers are created equal, and using a cheap, uncertified charger can affect both charging efficiency and battery health.

**The mistake:** Using bargain-bin chargers from market stalls or online sellers that do not meet Apple's specifications.

**What to do:**
- Use Apple's own charger or a reputable third-party charger with MFi certification
- Avoid extremely fast chargers that are not designed for your iPhone model, as excessive charging speeds generate more heat
- Check that your cable is in good condition, as frayed cables can cause inconsistent charging that stresses the battery

### Ignoring Software Updates

Apple regularly releases iOS updates that include battery optimisation improvements. Skipping updates means missing out on these enhancements.

**The mistake:** Putting off iOS updates indefinitely.

**What to do:** Keep your iPhone updated to the latest version of iOS. If you are worried about bugs in brand-new releases, waiting a week or two for any patches is reasonable, but do not skip updates entirely.
