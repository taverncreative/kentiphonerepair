## Should You Repair or Replace Your iPhone?

This is the question we get asked more than any other. Your iPhone is damaged, and you are standing at a crossroads: spend money on a repair, or put that money towards a new phone? There is no universal right answer, but by considering a few key factors, you can make a decision that makes financial and practical sense for your situation.

### The Cost Comparison

The most obvious factor is money. Let us look at the numbers honestly.

A screen repair on most iPhone models costs a fraction of the price of a new phone. Even a battery replacement is relatively inexpensive. Compare that to the cost of a new iPhone, which starts at several hundred pounds and can exceed a thousand pounds for Pro models.

**The repair makes financial sense when** the cost of fixing the phone is significantly less than the cost of replacing it. As a general rule, if the repair costs less than about a third of what you would spend on a comparable replacement, repairing is the better financial decision.

**Replacement makes more sense when** the phone needs multiple repairs that collectively approach or exceed the phone's current value. For example, if your iPhone 11 needs a new screen, a new battery, and a charging port repair, the combined cost might approach what you would pay for a good-condition refurbished iPhone 12 or 13.

However, remember that "replacing" does not necessarily mean buying brand new at full price. Refurbished phones and contract upgrades change the calculation. Similarly, trading in your damaged phone can offset some of the cost of a new one, though trade-in values for damaged phones are substantially lower.

### The Age of Your Device

The age of your iPhone is a crucial factor that is about more than just wear and tear.

**Software support.** Apple typically provides iOS updates for iPhones for five to six years after release. Once your phone drops off the supported list, it stops receiving security updates and eventually loses compatibility with newer apps. If your phone is near the end of its software support life, investing in a repair becomes less attractive.

**Performance.** Older iPhones naturally become slower as iOS and apps become more demanding. If your phone was already feeling sluggish before the damage occurred, a screen repair will not fix that underlying issue.

**Component availability.** As models get older, replacement parts can become harder to source and may be of lower quality. Repairs for very recent and very old models can sometimes be more expensive than those for models in the middle of their life cycle.

**The sweet spot for repair** is typically iPhones that are one to four years old. They are still well within their software support window, parts are readily available, and the cost differential between repair and replacement is strongly in favour of repair.

### Environmental Impact

This is something many people do not consider, but it is increasingly relevant.

Manufacturing a new iPhone has a significant environmental footprint. According to Apple's own environmental reports, the majority of an iPhone's lifetime carbon emissions come from its production, not from its years of use. Every new phone requires rare earth minerals, energy-intensive manufacturing, and global shipping.

Repairing your existing phone extends its useful life, delaying the need for a new device and reducing your personal contribution to electronic waste. A screen repair takes a phone that might otherwise end up in a drawer or a bin and gives it potentially years more life.

The numbers are striking. Extending the life of a smartphone by just one year can reduce its annual environmental impact by roughly 30 percent. If the environmental angle matters to you, repair is almost always the more responsible choice.

### Data and Convenience

Switching to a new phone is never as seamless as it should be. Even with iCloud backups and Apple's migration tools, there are always friction points:

- **App logins.** You will need to sign in to dozens of apps on the new device, and some apps require additional verification steps.
- **Two-factor authentication.** If your phone is your authentication device, setting up a new phone can be complicated if the old one is not working.
- **Payment cards.** Apple Pay cards need to be re-added and verified with your bank.
- **Settings and preferences.** Not everything transfers perfectly. Wallpapers, widget layouts, home screen arrangements, and app-specific settings may need manual reconfiguration.
- **Photos and messages.** While iCloud handles this well, large libraries can take hours or even days to fully sync to a new device.

A repair, by contrast, gives you back the exact phone you had before, with all your data, apps, and settings exactly as they were. There is no migration process, no setup time, and no risk of data loss.

### When You Should Definitely Repair

Repair is clearly the right choice when:

- The damage is limited to a single component (screen, battery, or charging port)
- The phone is less than four years old
- The phone was performing well before the damage
- The cost of repair is less than a third of the replacement cost
- You have the phone set up exactly how you want it
- You value minimising environmental waste

### When You Should Consider Replacing

Replacement is worth considering when:

- The phone needs multiple expensive repairs simultaneously
- The phone is more than five years old
- You were already experiencing performance issues before the damage
- Your phone is no longer receiving iOS updates
- The repair cost exceeds half the cost of a suitable replacement
- You have been wanting to upgrade for features your current phone lacks, such as a better camera or 5G connectivity

### The Middle Ground: Repair and Plan

There is a third option that many people overlook. Repair the phone now to get it working, then start saving for an upgrade on your own terms rather than being forced into an emergency purchase. This approach:

- Gets you back up and running immediately at minimal cost
- Gives you time to research your next phone properly
- Allows you to wait for sales, contract deals, or new model releases
- Means you are not making a major financial decision under pressure
- Lets you trade in a working phone later, which has a much higher trade-in value than a damaged one
