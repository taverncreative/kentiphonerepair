## What Causes iPhone Battery Swelling?

A swollen iPhone battery is one of the more alarming issues you can encounter with your device. If your iPhone screen is lifting away from the body, the case is bulging, or you can see a visible gap between the screen and the frame, you likely have a swollen battery. This is not something to ignore or work around. It is a safety concern that requires prompt attention.

## How Lithium-Ion Batteries Work

To understand swelling, it helps to know a little about the technology inside your iPhone. All modern smartphones use lithium-ion batteries because they offer an excellent balance of energy density, weight, and rechargeability.

Inside the battery, lithium ions move between two electrodes (the anode and cathode) through an electrolyte solution. When you charge the battery, ions move one direction. When you use the phone, they move the other way. This process is elegant and efficient, but the chemistry involved is not without its risks.

## What Causes a Battery to Swell?

Swelling occurs when gas builds up inside the battery's sealed casing. This gas is a byproduct of chemical reactions that should not be happening inside a healthy battery. Several factors can trigger this process.

### Age and wear

The most common cause is simply age. As a lithium-ion battery goes through hundreds of charge cycles, the internal chemistry gradually degrades. The electrolyte can begin to decompose, producing gas as a byproduct. Most iPhone batteries are designed to retain 80% of their capacity after 500 complete charge cycles, but degradation continues beyond that point, and the risk of swelling increases.

### Overcharging and heat

Repeatedly charging your iPhone in hot conditions accelerates chemical degradation. Leaving your phone on a car dashboard in summer, charging it under a pillow, or using it intensively while it is plugged in can all generate excessive heat that stresses the battery chemistry. Over time, this thermal stress can trigger gas production.

### Physical damage

If your iPhone has been dropped or subjected to impact, the battery can suffer internal damage even if the phone looks fine externally. A dent or compression of the battery cell can compromise the separator between the electrodes, leading to abnormal chemical reactions and gas build-up.

### Manufacturing defects

In rare cases, a battery may have a manufacturing defect that makes it prone to swelling. This is uncommon with Apple's quality control, but no manufacturing process is perfect.

### Using non-certified chargers

Cheap, non-certified chargers can deliver inconsistent or excessive voltage to your battery. Over time, this can cause damage to the battery chemistry that leads to swelling. Always use MFi-certified or Apple-supplied charging accessories.

## Warning Signs of a Swelling Battery

Catching a swelling battery early is important for both your safety and the health of your phone. Here are the signs to watch for.

- **Screen lifting.** The most obvious sign. If your iPhone screen is starting to separate from the body, particularly along one edge, a swelling battery is the most likely cause.
- **Visible bulge.** In more advanced cases, you can see and feel a bulge in the back of the phone or along the sides.
- **Rocking on flat surfaces.** If your phone used to sit flat on a table and now rocks slightly, the battery may be beginning to swell.
- **Cracking sounds.** Internal adhesives and components under pressure from a swelling battery can produce faint cracking or popping sounds.
- **Overheating.** A swelling battery often runs hotter than normal during use and charging.
- **Rapid battery drain.** A compromised battery cannot hold charge effectively, so you may notice significantly shorter battery life.
- **Unexpected shutdowns.** The phone may power off suddenly, even when showing a reasonable charge percentage.

## The Dangers of a Swollen Battery

A swollen battery is not just a performance issue; it is a potential safety hazard.

### Fire and thermal runaway

In extreme cases, a swollen lithium-ion battery can enter thermal runaway, a self-sustaining reaction that generates intense heat and can cause the battery to catch fire or, very rarely, explode. While this is uncommon, the risk is real enough to take seriously.

### Chemical exposure

If a swollen battery ruptures, it can release toxic and corrosive chemicals. These can damage your skin and eyes on contact and produce harmful fumes.

### Damage to other components

As the battery swells, it puts pressure on surrounding components. The screen, logic board, cameras, and connecting cables can all be damaged by a battery that is expanding beyond its designed dimensions.

## What to Do If Your iPhone Battery Is Swelling

If you suspect or confirm that your iPhone battery is swelling, take these steps.

### Stop using the phone

This is not a situation where you can carry on and deal with it later. Continued use generates heat, which can accelerate the swelling and increase the risk of more serious problems.

### Do not charge it

Charging a swelling battery pumps more energy into an already compromised system. Disconnect any charger and do not plug it back in.

### Do not try to puncture or compress the battery

This should go without saying, but never attempt to relieve the pressure by puncturing the battery or pressing the screen back down. Puncturing a lithium-ion battery can cause an immediate fire.

### Store it safely

Place the phone on a non-flammable surface away from combustible materials. A metal tray or a stone countertop is ideal. Do not leave it in a bag, on a bed, or near paper and fabric.

### Seek professional replacement promptly

A swollen battery needs to be removed and replaced by a trained technician with the proper tools and safety procedures. This is not a DIY job. The battery needs to be handled carefully to avoid puncture or further stress during removal.

## Prevention: How to Keep Your Battery Healthy

While you cannot prevent battery ageing entirely, you can take steps to maximise your battery's lifespan and reduce the risk of swelling.

- **Avoid extreme heat.** Do not leave your phone in direct sunlight, in a hot car, or charge it under blankets and pillows.
- **Use quality chargers.** Stick with Apple-supplied or MFi-certified charging accessories.
- **Avoid full discharge cycles.** Regularly letting your battery drain to zero accelerates degradation. Try to keep it between 20% and 80% for optimal longevity.
- **Enable Optimised Battery Charging.** This iOS feature learns your routine and slows charging at 80% when it predicts you will not need a full charge immediately, reducing stress on the battery.
- **Replace ageing batteries proactively.** If your battery health has dropped below 80%, consider replacing it before problems develop. A fresh battery is far cheaper than repairing collateral damage from a swollen one.
