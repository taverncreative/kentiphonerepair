## iPhone Screen Not Responding After Drop

You have dropped your iPhone. You pick it up, and the screen is either completely unresponsive or only partially working. Your heart sinks. This is one of the most stressful moments for any smartphone owner, especially when your entire life is stored on that device. The good news is that in many cases, an unresponsive screen after a drop is repairable. Here is what you need to know.

### What to Do Immediately

Before you panic, there are a few things worth trying right away. Sometimes an unresponsive screen after a drop is caused by a temporary issue rather than permanent damage.

**Step 1: Check for visible damage.** Look carefully at the screen. Is the glass cracked? Is there discolouration, black spots, or lines running across the display? The type of visible damage gives clues about what has happened internally.

**Step 2: Try a force restart.** A drop can sometimes cause the software to freeze or crash, making the screen appear unresponsive even though the hardware is fine. To force restart:
- On iPhone 8 and later: Quickly press and release the Volume Up button, then quickly press and release the Volume Down button, then press and hold the Side button until the Apple logo appears.
- On iPhone 7: Press and hold both the Volume Down and Side buttons simultaneously until the Apple logo appears.

If the phone restarts and the screen works normally again, the issue was likely a software crash caused by the impact rather than physical damage.

**Step 3: Check if only part of the screen is unresponsive.** Sometimes a drop damages the digitiser in a specific area, meaning the screen displays content fine but does not register touch in certain zones. Try tapping different areas of the screen to map out where touch does and does not work.

### Understanding Display vs Digitiser Damage

Your iPhone screen is actually made up of several layers, and understanding which layer is damaged helps determine the repair approach.

**The display (LCD or OLED)** is responsible for producing the image you see. If this layer is damaged, you may notice:
- Black spots or blotches on the screen
- Lines running horizontally or vertically across the display
- Colour distortion or a greenish tint
- Part of the screen appearing completely black while the rest works
- Flickering or dimming in certain areas

**The digitiser** is the layer that detects your touch input. If this layer is damaged, you may notice:
- The screen displays content perfectly but does not respond to touch
- Touch only works in certain areas of the screen
- Ghost touches, where the phone registers inputs you are not making
- Delayed or inaccurate touch response

In many cases, both layers are affected simultaneously, but it is possible for one to be damaged while the other remains functional. Either way, the repair typically involves replacing the entire screen assembly, which includes both the display and digitiser as an integrated unit.

### When the Screen Is Fixable

The vast majority of unresponsive screens after a drop are repairable. Here are the scenarios where a repair is likely to be straightforward:

- **Cracked glass with display issues:** The most common scenario. The screen assembly is replaced as a complete unit, restoring both the display and touch functionality.
- **Loose connector:** Sometimes the impact of a drop can partially dislodge the screen's ribbon cable from the logic board. A technician can reseat this connection, which may resolve the issue without needing a new screen.
- **Digitiser-only damage:** Even if the display looks fine, a damaged digitiser still requires a screen replacement, but the repair is standard and typically completed in under an hour.
- **Minor internal displacement:** The drop may have shifted internal components slightly. A professional can assess and correct this.

### When It Might Be More Serious

In some cases, an unresponsive screen is a symptom of deeper damage:

- **Logic board damage:** If the drop was severe, the main circuit board inside the phone can be cracked or have components knocked loose. This is more complex and expensive to repair, though not always impossible.
- **Damaged connectors or cables:** If the ribbon cables that connect the screen to the phone's internals are torn rather than simply dislodged, the repair may be more involved.
- **Frame damage:** A severe drop can bend the phone's chassis, which complicates screen replacement because the new screen may not seat properly in a bent frame.
- **Multiple component failures:** If the screen, battery, and other components are all damaged simultaneously, the cost of repair may approach or exceed the value of the phone.

### What Not to Do

While waiting for a repair, avoid these common mistakes:

- **Do not keep trying to use a partially working screen.** Pressing hard on a cracked or damaged screen can push glass fragments into the phone's internals and cause additional damage.
- **Do not attempt to open the phone yourself.** Modern iPhones are sealed with adhesive and require specialist tools. Opening the phone incorrectly can damage waterproofing seals, tear cables, or crack the screen further.
- **Do not ignore ghost touches.** If your phone is registering touches on its own, it could accidentally dial numbers, send messages, or make changes to your settings. If possible, power the phone off until it can be repaired.
- **Do not put off the repair.** A cracked screen with an exposed digitiser is vulnerable to moisture and debris ingress. What starts as a screen repair can become a more expensive problem if left untreated.
